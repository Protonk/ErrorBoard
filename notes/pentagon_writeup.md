# Pentagon inspection findings — narrative writeup

Companion to the auto-generated tables in `notes/pentagon_findings.md` (regenerated by `python -m errorboard.pentagon`). Per-vertex full dumps live at `runs/pentagon/V*_full.txt`; raw arrays at `runs/pentagon/V*.npz`.

The pentagon: five HookedTransformer-bridged checkpoints chosen to span two axes — training trajectory (V1→V2→V3) at fixed architecture (L4-E044, seed 0) and architecture variation (V3, V4, V5) at iter 20000. See `code/errorboard/pentagon.py` for the exact checkpoints.

Probes (all on a 64-sample batch from the cancellation regime, plus a 31-sample doubling holdout for Park Thm 5):

1. Attention pattern at result-predictor positions (18..25 in the 27-token autoregressive input)
2. Direct logit attribution by block at the correct result-bit class
3. Commutativity `f(a, b) ?= f(b, a)` and operand-a/b residual cosine similarity on `a == b` inputs

---

## Finding 1 — commutativity is learned and saturates at V4

Full-result commutativity (all 8 result bits agreeing under operand swap):

| metric | V1 (5k) | V2 (10k) | V3 (20k) | V4 (E128) | V5 (L1) |
|--------|--------:|---------:|---------:|----------:|--------:|
| full-result agreement | 0.33 | 0.64 | 0.91 | **1.00** | 0.91 |

The V1→V3 monotone climb is the cleanest "watch the algorithm form" result in the run. V4 (more width, same training) reaches 100%; V5 (depth-1, same width as V4) plateaus at 91%, identical to V3.

The slowest-emerging bits are `e0` and `e1` (exponent low-order). The fastest is `m0`, already 100% at V1. This is mechanistically sensible: exponent processing involves the alignment step (shift the smaller-exponent operand's mantissa right by `Δexp` bits), which is operand-asymmetric — the model has to learn to handle this asymmetry symmetrically with respect to operand order. Sign and `e3` show a transient dip at V2-V3 before resolving — possibly local optima during training that get smoothed out by V4's larger capacity.

**Solid claim:** commutativity emerges with training and is fully attained at V4.

**Hedged claim:** the depth-1 model (V5) appears to have a structural ceiling at ~91% commutativity. We have one data point. To make this load-bearing, we'd want a V5-analog at a different seed or width.

## Finding 2 — Park Thm 5 is learned for 7/8 bits, but m0 violates it *more* with capability

Park's Theorem 5 says any FP transformer must preserve equality: if input columns `i` and `j` are identical, output columns at `i` and `j` are identical. Strict reading of this is broken in our setup by positional embeddings (PE adds different vectors to columns at different positions). The probe asks: does the model *learn to recover* the equality, layer by layer?

Final-block operand-a/b residual cosine similarity on doubling (`a == b`) inputs:

| bit | V1 | V2 | V3 | V4 | V5 |
|-----|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sign | +0.97 | +0.98 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 |
| e3 | +0.97 | +0.98 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.98 |
| e2 | +0.98 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 |
| e1 | +0.96 | +0.97 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.96 |
| e0 | +0.98 | +0.98 | +0.99 | +0.98 | +0.99 |
| m2 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +1.00 |
| m1 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.99 | +0.98 | +1.00 |
| **m0** | **+0.41** | **+0.58** | **+0.39** | **+0.24** | **−0.03** |

For 7 of 8 bits, the model approximately satisfies Park Thm 5 already at V1. The m0 anomaly is the substantive finding: cosine similarity at m0 is low everywhere and **gets worse with capability** — bigger model + more training pushes residuals at m0 positions farther apart, not closer together.

### What the targeted probe actually showed

After running the m0 attention probe (`code/errorboard/m0_probe.py`, full results in `notes/m0_probe_findings.md`), the data confirms the model treats c:m0 prediction differently from c:m1 and c:m2 — but the mechanism I originally proposed below was wrong on the math.

**What the probe finds.** Normalized attention from each mantissa-bit predictor over the 6 operand mantissa positions, doubling regime:

| target | V1 | V2 | V3 | V4 | V5 |
|--------|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| c:m0 → a:m0 | 0.36 | 0.32 | 0.29 | **0.21** | 0.22 |
| c:m0 → a:m1 | 0.11 | 0.12 | 0.14 | **0.21** | 0.09 |
| c:m1 → a:m1 (natural) | 0.21 | 0.19 | 0.21 | **0.27** | 0.16 |
| c:m2 → a:m2 (natural) | 0.21 | 0.20 | 0.20 | **0.17** | 0.18 |

At V4 (saturated), c:m1 attends most to operand m1 and c:m2 attends most to operand m2 — natural per-bit correspondence. The c:m0 predictor does **not** follow this: attention is roughly equal on a:m0 (0.21) and a:m1 (0.21). So m0 is genuinely treated differently in the saturated model. The V1→V4 trajectory reweights c:m0 attention *away from* a:m0 and *toward* a:m1. On cancellation regime: a:m0 attention falls 0.42 → 0.24, a:m1 attention grows 0.11 → 0.17.

**What I got wrong below.** The original framing claimed "for doubling, result m0 ← operand m1." That's incorrect for the common case (normal-normal doubling produces `1.m2m1m0 × 2^(e+1)` — same mantissa, just an exponent bump). I was confusing FP doubling with binary-integer doubling.

**Revised reading (still hedged).** The probe shows: (a) m0 prediction *is* qualitatively different from m1/m2 prediction at saturation, (b) capability increases broaden where c:m0 attention lands. Why this happens has at least three plausible explanations that aren't mutually exclusive:

- **Rounding-bit propagation:** when operands have different exponents, the smaller-exp mantissa gets shifted right; bits below the resulting LSB form guard/round/sticky used in RNE. Operand m1 ends up in the result m0 slot for a 1-bit alignment shift.
- **Subnormal renormalization:** subnormal sums can cross into normal range, which involves leading-zero detection and a mantissa shift — distributing operand m1 info into the result m0 position for those cases.
- **Generic "LSB is hardest" effect:** the LSB has the tightest constraint, so the model may distribute attention more broadly simply because it needs more information to be correct, not because of any specific arithmetic mechanism.

The probe rules out "the model is just doing per-bit lookup" (it isn't, for m0). It doesn't distinguish among these three on its own. The follow-up Δexp slice below partially does.

### Δexp slice — H1 substantively supported at V4

The Δexp-conditioned probe (`code/errorboard/m0_dexp.py`, results in `notes/m0_dexp_findings.md`) stratifies normal-normal pairs by `Δexp = |unbiased_exp(a) − unbiased_exp(b)|` (excluding subnormals, zeros, NaN) and re-runs the c:m0 attention breakdown.

c:m0 attention ratio `(a:m1 + b:m1) / (a:m0 + b:m0)` by (Δexp bucket × vertex), 64 samples per bucket:

| Δexp | V1 | V2 | V3 | V4 | V5 |
|-----:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.39 | 0.47 | 0.51 | 0.75 | 0.50 |
| 1 | 0.39 | 0.46 | 0.51 | **0.84** | 0.47 |
| 2 | 0.41 | 0.48 | 0.56 | **0.84** | 0.52 |
| 3 | 0.43 | 0.50 | 0.60 | **0.82** | 0.55 |
| 4-7 | 0.39 | 0.48 | 0.59 | 0.67 | 0.46 |
| 8+ | 0.44 | 0.51 | 0.60 | 0.69 | 0.46 |

Three readings:

- **At V4, the ratio is genuinely Δexp-dependent** — plateau at 0.82-0.84 for Δexp ∈ {1, 2, 3} (small alignment shifts where operand m1 can land in the result rounding region), dropping to 0.67-0.69 for Δexp ≥ 4 (alignment shifts that push the smaller operand's mantissa below the LSB; it can only contribute as a sticky bit). The strict H1 prediction ("peak at Δexp=1") was too narrow — the actual shape is "elevated across the alignment-relevant range, drops where alignment can no longer move operand m1 into the result LSB neighborhood." **H1 substantively supported in modified form.**
- **V3 (borderline) has a different Δexp shape** — ratio rises monotonically with Δexp (0.51 → 0.60). So the alignment-aware attention is a property of saturation, not just capability. V3 has a coarser heuristic ("for hard cases use operand m1 more"); V4 refines it into something Δexp-specific.
- **V5 has essentially no Δexp dependence** (0.46-0.55 across all buckets). Confirms V5 isn't doing alignment-shift reasoning at all — its operand-b asymmetry from the prior probe is a single broadly-applied heuristic.

**One additional finding from the slice:** c:m0 → b:m0 attention *grows* with Δexp at V4 (0.076 at Δexp=0 → 0.103 at Δexp≥8). For large Δexp, the result's m0 is dominated by the larger-magnitude operand — so this could be a "magnitude-comparison and copy" circuit for the large-Δexp case, separate from the alignment-rounding circuit for small Δexp. Two distinct sub-circuits within c:m0 prediction. Speculative; held lightly.

**What's still open after the slice:**

- **H2 (subnormal renormalization)** wasn't tested — we filtered subnormals out. A separate slice stratifying by `is_subnormal_result` would address it. The pentagon's m0 cos-sim divergence is averaged across both normal-result and subnormal-result doubling cases; H2 could account for some of the divergence independent of H1.
- **The vertex-wide ratio increase** (V1 → V4 at every Δexp) is broader than the H1 shape and may reflect a generic LSB-is-hard effect (H3) running in parallel with H1. The two aren't mutually exclusive.

**Original wrong reading, left for the record.** In FP8 addition, the result's m0 (LSB) is determined by rounding, which uses bits *below* the operand m0 (guard/round/sticky). For doubling (`a + a = 2a`), I claimed the result's m0 is determined by the operand's m1 — not its m0. This is wrong for normal-normal doubling (result mantissa = operand mantissa; only the exponent bumps).

**Bonus finding — V5 is operand-asymmetric.** For c:m2 on cancellation, V5 attends 0.28 to b:m2 vs 0.18 to a:m2. For c:m0 on cancellation, V5 attends 0.30 to b:m0 vs 0.23 to a:m0. **Depth-1 produces not just diffuse attention but *asymmetric* attention, biased toward operand b.** Consistent with the 91% commutativity ceiling at V5: the asymmetry would naturally cause some f(a,b) ≠ f(b,a) cases. The pattern is absent at V1-V4, all of which have 4 layers — suggests depth provides the apparatus to enforce operand symmetry. Held lightly (one data point, one architecture).

**Caveat on the cosine-sim numbers:** the doubling holdout has only 31 samples. Each cell carries ~±0.05 noise. The *pattern* (m0 is the outlier) is robust; the *exact* cosine values are not. The m0-probe cancellation results use the 64-sample batch and have tighter precision.

## Finding 3 — final-block dominance grows with training

Direct logit attribution summed across the 8 result bits:

| stage | V1 | V2 | V3 |
|-------|---:|---:|---:|
| embed | −1.6 | −0.6 | −0.1 |
| block 0 | +10.1 | +10.2 | +9.5 |
| block 1 | +7.5 | +5.4 | +6.5 |
| block 2 | +7.9 | +7.7 | +10.4 |
| **block 3** | **+36.6** | **+44.7** | **+48.0** |

Block 3's contribution grows monotonically across the V1→V3 training trajectory. Blocks 0-2 are roughly stable (none growing, some shrinking). The algorithmic work "migrates into" the final layer as training progresses.

V4 (same depth, more width) extends this: block 3 reaches +50.6 and block 0 contributes +21.9 (more width gives early layers more room to contribute as well).

**Solid claim:** in this architecture family, block 3 carries the majority of the correct-class logit, and its share grows with training.

**Open question:** is this a structural property of the algorithm (FP addition really does need a final integration step that early blocks can't perform), or an emergent feature of how the optimizer fills capacity (it could equally well have distributed work evenly, but didn't)? Distinguishing requires an ablation: remove block 3, retrain — does block 0 grow to compensate?

## Finding 4 — V5 may substitute magnitude for sharpness *(held lightly)*

V5 (depth-1) has just one transformer block, so the same DLA framing doesn't apply cleanly. But two observables stand out side-by-side with V4 (same width, more depth):

| observable | V4 (L=4, E=128) | V5 (L=1, E=128) |
|------------|----------------:|----------------:|
| total correct-class logit | +80.5 | +112.1 |
| final-block contribution | +50.6 (block 3) | +97.3 (only block) |
| attention sharpness (mean max attn at result positions) | 0.306 | 0.150 |
| default accuracy | 99.7% | 94.9% |
| commutativity full-agreement | 1.00 | 0.91 |

The pattern: V5 emits higher correct-class logits despite lower accuracy, with much more diffuse attention. The intuitive reading — *V5 can't form sharp attention patterns without depth, so it compensates by piling magnitude onto whatever positions it does pay attention to* — fits this data and aligns with the general "depth lets attention be selective" prior.

**Why this is hedged.** The data is consistent with this reading but doesn't isolate the mechanism. Alternatives that fit the same observations:

- The pattern might be a property of training-from-scratch a 1-layer model with cross-entropy, not specifically about depth. A wider 1-layer model might be sharper without needing depth.
- "Magnitude vs sharpness" is presented as a trade-off but we haven't shown a *causal* link — only co-occurrence in one architecture.
- The correct-class logit magnitude is on an unbounded scale; high logits can co-exist with high error rates if the *gap* between correct and wrong class is what matters, not absolute magnitude. We haven't measured that gap.
- Diffuse attention may itself be a *consequence* of the model trying many possible decompositions in parallel (one layer has to do everything), not a *cause* of compensation.

The framing "substitutes magnitude for sharpness" is a useful intuitive handle, and the V5 observation is consistent with it, but we should not treat the claim as established. A clean falsification: a 1-layer model with much more width should, if the depth-causes-diffuseness theory is right, *stay* diffuse — width alone shouldn't sharpen attention. An L1-E256 cell would test this.

## Finding 5 — V3 vs V5: same accuracy, different mechanism

Both V3 (L4-E044 borderline) and V5 (L1-E128 depth-capped) sit at borderline default accuracy (~95-96%) with comparable commutativity (91%). They differ on:

| observable | V3 | V5 |
|------------|---:|---:|
| attention sharpness | 0.265 | 0.150 |
| m0 residual sim | +0.39 | −0.03 |
| block structure | 4 blocks with characteristic specialization | 1 block doing everything |
| commutativity | 91% (rising; V4 at 100% shows headroom) | 91% (capped; no further capacity) |

V3 is "algorithm forming, just shy of completion" — V4 shows where V3 is heading with more capacity. V5 is "different algorithm entirely" — the residual-equality at m0 is gone (cos = −0.03, near-orthogonal), and the attention is uniformly diffuse where V3's is structured.

**Solid claim:** at matched accuracy, V3 and V5 implement notably different internal structures. The borderline state is not a single thing.

**Practical reading:** if you're deploying a small arithmetic model at 95% accuracy and want predictable error structure, depth-bottlenecked (V5-like) and capacity-bottlenecked (V3-like) models are not interchangeable, even though their accuracy numbers match.

## Caveats spanning the whole pass

- **Single regime.** All probes ran on 64 cancellation-regime samples + 31 doubling samples. The per-block specialization and attention patterns may differ for other regimes (default, large-Δexp, etc.). The findings on commutativity and Thm 5 are likely regime-independent (they involve operand swapping, not the addition itself), but specifically the DLA "block 0 does mantissa, block 2 does exponent" intuition from the V3 single-vertex run is regime-conditional — we haven't shown the pattern holds outside cancellation.

- **Single seed.** Pentagon uses seed-0 of each cell. The variance analysis (`notes/pentagon_findings.md` predecessor work) showed seeds within a cell look algorithmically similar, but a different seed could shift the layer-specialization layout. The findings are descriptions of *these checkpoints*, not architecture-level claims.

- **31-sample doubling holdout.** Thm 5 residual cosine similarities have ±0.05 cell-wise noise. The m0 anomaly is robust at that resolution (the cells are 0.4-0.6 standard deviations apart from the cluster mean); the precise values are not.

- **One probe set.** Three probes were run; many more are possible. Causal validation (ablation of specific heads/blocks) hasn't happened, which means everything reported above is correlational evidence consistent with the proposed mechanisms — not direct mechanistic identification.
